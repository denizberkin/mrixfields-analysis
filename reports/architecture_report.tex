\section*{What the log actually says}

\textbf{Three architecture changes were worth nothing, two data changes were worth everything.}
Swapping BatchNorm/ReLU for InstanceNorm/LeakyReLU moves the score by $0.00007$
(\texttt{unet-vanilla} vs \texttt{unet-unconditioned}). A shifted-window transformer, a
DINOv3 ViT and a self-supervised tubelet encoder are all stronger feature extractors than a
32-channel U-Net, and all three land $0.02$--$0.05$ \emph{below} it. What moved the number was
telling the network which field pair it is solving ($+0.0278$), showing it 1{,}939 volumes it
could not otherwise use ($+0.0103$), and giving it the other two contrasts ($+0.0037$).

\smallskip
\textbf{With three paired subjects, capacity is not the constraint --- information is.}

\smallskip
\textbf{Every change was made a no-op at initialisation.} The residual head, the FiLM
projections and the three new input channels are all zero-initialised, so each run
\emph{starts} at exactly the previous run's score and any movement is attributable to the
change rather than to a different random init. The 4-channel model reproduced its
1-channel parent at $\max|\Delta| = 0$ on real data before training began.

\noindent
\textbf{The all-time leaderboard is misleading, and it is worth knowing why.} Its top three (0.9314, 0.9281,
0.9263, all with nRMSE near $0.11$ against the field's $\sim0.23$) come from two teams inside a
five-week window that closed on 2026-07-07. \emph{Every} submission in the challenge scoring
nRMSE below $0.15$ falls in that window; since then the best anyone has posted, those two teams
included, is $0.2151$.

The evaluator did not change underneath them: four different teams have submitted an unmodified
copy of the source, and all four scored \textbf{0.836497 / 0.521604} to six decimals, on
2026-06-16, 08-19, 08-20 and 08-28. That fingerprint pins the metric as stable across the whole
period, so the discontinuity is in those submissions, not in the scoring. One of them is even
named \texttt{new\_no\_leak}. Treat $\sim$\,0.9167 as the real frontier and the gap we have to
close as \textbf{about 0.010}, not 0.025.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{What survives that correction is the shape of our error.} We are 3rd of 23 on LPIPS and
15th on nRMSE. The five teams above us are better on \emph{both} SSIM and nRMSE while being worse
than us on LPIPS. We train on $L_1 + 0.05\,\mathrm{LPIPS}$ and no structural term at all --- we
are optimising the metric we already lead and not the one that is ranked.
