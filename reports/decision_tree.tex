\vspace{-2mm}
{\small
\textbf{How to read the stubs.} Every grey branch was measured, not guessed, and most were
killed by a number rather than by taste. Three of them (\S11, \S15, \S20) are real but too
small: $+0.0008$ to $+0.0011$ local, against a seed noise floor of $\pm0.001$ (\S16) and a
between-subject spread of $0.011$ (\S14.1) --- effects that cannot be told apart from noise on
three training subjects should not spend a submission slot.

\textbf{One branch is on the spine but should be a stub.} \S9's FiLM+residual head scored
\emph{below} the plain conditional U-Net it replaced ($-0.0048$), and \S17's 60-epoch run
confirmed it converges to a worse optimum. It survives only because the pretraining line was
built on that checkpoint rather than on the plain one, so every score from 0.901633 down carries
a component whose one controlled comparison says it costs $\approx0.005$. Re-running the
pretrain$\rightarrow$fine-tune line without it is the cheapest untested gain on the board.

\textbf{What the shape of the tree says.} Every stub on the right is an \emph{architecture} or a
\emph{post-hoc trick}. Every step on the spine after the U-Net exists is a change in what the
model gets to \emph{see}: which domains it is mapping between, 1{,}939 volumes with no paired
target, and the other two contrasts of the same subject. With three paired subjects that is the
binding constraint, and it is why the next two experiments are a loss term aimed at the ranked
metric and a schedule question --- with synthetic paired data as the one lever left that
attacks the constraint itself.}
